Case Study

Milo Tyres Ltd.

Navigating Growth and Headwinds in the Karnataka Tyre Aftermarket — FY2025 & FY2026

1. Company Background

Milo Tyres Ltd. is a mid-sized Indian tyre manufacturer founded in 2003 and headquartered in Pune, Maharashtra, with its primary manufacturing campus in Nashik. The company produces a broad portfolio spanning two-wheeler tyres, passenger-car radials (PCR), light-commercial-vehicle (LCV) tyres, and select truck-bus radials (TBR). Milo entered the Karnataka aftermarket in a meaningful way starting FY2023, targeting the state as a key growth geography given its large urbanised base, robust logistics network, and rapid vehicle-fleet expansion.

With an annual revenue of approximately ₹2,400 crore (FY2025) and a pan-India dealer network of roughly 6,800 outlets, Milo occupies a challenger position in the domestic tyre industry — behind established leaders MRF, Apollo, and CEAT but ahead of several regional players. The company's stated ambition for Karnataka is to grow its aftermarket share from 7% in FY2025 to 9% by the close of FY2026.

2. Karnataka Tyre Aftermarket — Overview

Karnataka is India's third-largest state tyre aftermarket by value, trailing only Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu. The state benefits from a high density of commercial vehicles on national and state highways, a maturing personal-vehicle fleet in Bengaluru, and growing agri/construction activity in its northern and coastal belts.

PCR (passenger car radials) represents the highest-growth and highest-margin segment in urban Karnataka. Bengaluru's premium-car ownership expansion has driven demand for 16–18-inch high performance tyres — a sub-segment where Milo has yet to establish a credible SKU portfolio or brand positioning.

The TBR segment, though smaller by transaction count, is accelerating sharply as e-commerce and FMCG logistics companies expand their last-mile fleets into Tier-2 Karnataka cities. In FY2026, TBR is expected to be the fastest-growing segment at ~10%.

MRF's dominance stems from decades of brand building and an extensive network of Authorised Retreading & Service centres (ARSC) spread across Karnataka's 31 districts. Apollo's strength in TBR is bolstered by fleet management contracts with logistics aggregators headquartered in Bengaluru. CEAT has been aggressively expanding its quick-fit tyre-changing franchise ("CEAT Shoppe") in Tier-2 cities, competing directly with Milo's dealer base.

Bengaluru Urban drives premium demand and sets pricing benchmarks. However, Milo's current network density (38 active dealers in Bengaluru vs. MRF's 120+) severely limits its ability to capture impulse replacement purchases. In contrast, North Karnataka presents a paradox: Milo has better distributor penetration in volume terms but faces margin erosion due to distributor conflicts introduced when competing sub-distributors were appointed simultaneously in FY2024.

Coastal Karnataka's OHT sub-segment (tyres for trawlers, agri-machinery) remains largely unaddressed by Milo's current product portfolio, representing both a gap and a medium-term opportunity.

6. Key Challenges Faced by Milo

FY2025 Challenges

  • Critical Premium PCR Gap in Bengaluru: Milo's PCR portfolio topped out at 15-inch radials, leaving the fast-growing ≥16-inch UHP (ultra-high-performance) segment entirely to MRF, Bridgestone, and Continental. Bengaluru accounted for over 60% of Karnataka's PCR aftermarket by value in FY2025.
  • High Distributor Conflicts in North Karnataka: Dual appointment of two competing sub-distributors in Hubballi and Belagavi led to price undercutting, grey market diversion, and dealer attrition. Three primary dealers switched to CEAT during Q3 FY2025.
  • High Brand Recall Deficit: Unaided brand recall for Milo in Karnataka stood at ~11% vs. MRF's 68% and CEAT's 34% in a consumer survey conducted in January 2025. Milo's media spend in the state was ₹4.2 crore against MRF's estimated ₹22 crore.
  • Medium Raw Material Supply Disruptions: Elevated natural rubber prices (up 18% YoY in H1 FY2025) and a logistics bottleneck at Nhava Sheva port impacted Milo's Karnataka-bound inventory. Stockouts in the LCV segment during Q2 FY2025 resulted in lost sales of approximately ₹28 crore.
  • Medium Workshop & Quick-Fit Channel Underpenetration: Only 8% of Milo's Karnataka dealers had a quick-fit bay, vs. an industry average of 24%. Consumers increasingly prefer integrated tyre-fitting experiences, particularly in urban areas.

FY2026 Challenges

  • Critical EV Tyre Segment Emergence: Bengaluru's growing EV fleet (two-wheeler and passenger car) requires specialised tyres with higher load ratings, lower rolling resistance, and reinforced sidewalls. Milo has no EV-rated tyre in its Karnataka catalogue as of FY2026 Q1, ceding a nascent but rapidly growing sub-segment to Bridgestone and newly entered Yokohama.
  • Critical Digital Channel & D2C Competition: Platforms such as Tyre Kart and TyreSeal have gained traction in Bengaluru, offering transparent pricing and home fitment. Milo lacks a consumer-facing digital storefront and has no integration with these platforms, making it invisible to the online-first buyer segment (estimated 18% of PCR replacements in urban Karnataka in FY2026).
  • High Price Pressure from Chinese Imports: Anti-dumping duty exemptions on selected tyre categories have enabled Chinese brands (Linglong, Triangle, Doublestar) to undercut Milo's LCV and 2W pricing by 12–18% in Tier-2 and rural Karnataka. Milo's LCV segment — its strongest Karnataka vertical — faces volume erosion.
  • High Share Gain Target vs. Execution Capacity: The stated goal of growing from 7% to 9% share in FY2026 requires incremental revenue of approximately ₹69 crore at current market size projections. Achieving this requires 40 new dealer appointments, a ₹6 crore incremental marketing budget, and resolution of North Karnataka channel conflict — all simultaneously.
  • Medium Monsoon Season Demand Volatility in Coastal Belt: Coastal Karnataka's two-wheeler and LCV demand drops sharply (–22% in Q2) due to monsoon disruption of fishing and rural commerce. Milo's relatively thin distributor margins in this zone make it difficult to incentivise dealers to maintain adequate inventory buffers.

7. Strategic Outlook

Milo's management has identified the following priority actions for FY2026, acknowledging that Karnataka represents a proving ground for its broader pan-India challenger strategy:

  1. Launch UHP PCR Range: A new 16–18-inch PCR line (project codename "Apex") is scheduled for H1 FY2026 market launch, targeting Bengaluru premium dealers and digital platforms simultaneously.
  2. Resolve North Karnataka Channel Structure: Terminate the conflicting dual-distributor arrangement and appoint a single state-level super-distributor for North Karnataka by end of Q1 FY2026.
  3. Digital Marketplace Integration: Onboard Milo's catalogue onto Tyre Kart and TyreSeal; develop a dealer-locator and booking widget for the Milo website.
  4. EV Tyre Pilot: Source and white-label EV-rated 2W and PCR tyres for Karnataka as a bridge strategy while Milo's own EV-rated production line ramps up in FY2027.
  5. Quick-Fit Bay Expansion: Target 30 quick-fit-enabled dealers in Karnataka by March 2026, anchored in Bengaluru and the Mysuru belt.

Execution risk remains high. Milo's Karnataka General Manager has flagged that simultaneously managing a product launch, channel restructuring, and digital integration stretches the state sales team, which currently has eight field executives for a 31-district geography. Organisational bandwidth, rather than strategy, may be the binding constraint in FY2026.
